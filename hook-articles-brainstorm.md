## The idea

Everything currently published is a 4–6 minute pillar-support spoke: broad scope, decision-maker audience, "here's the whole picture." The marketing company's point is that there's no shallow end. Nothing that gets read in 90 seconds, gets shared in Slack, or ranks for the long-tail question someone actually typed at 11pm.

**Hook articles are a different unit:** 400–800 words, 2–3 min read, one narrow answer, numbered or checklist format. They are cheap to write, easy to link *from* (every hook points up to its parent spoke), and they catch search traffic the long pieces are too broad to win.

### Rules for this format

1. **One question, one answer.** If it needs a "it depends" section, it's a spoke, not a hook.
2. **Numbered or named.** "Top 5," "The 3 things," "What breaks first." The number is the hook.
3. **Every hook links up.** Each one ends pointing at the full spoke. That's the whole SEO argument for doing them.
4. **2–3 min read time** in `insights.yml`, so the hub visually signals the difference.
5. **Write from tickets, not from theory.** The best ones are literally "things we fix over and over."

---

## Format patterns that work

| Pattern | Why it hooks | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *Top N things to do after [event]* | Reader has just had the event | "5 things to do with a Mac after an employee leaves" |
| *The N most common [X] mistakes* | Fear of already having made it | "The 6 most common Apple Business Manager setup mistakes" |
| *What breaks first when [scale event]* | Reader recognises their own growth | "What breaks first when your office hits 25 Macs" |
| *N questions to ask before [purchase]* | Pre-purchase, high intent | "7 questions to ask an MSP before you sign" |
| *[Thing] vs [Thing] in 60 seconds* | Comparison, fast | "Apple Business Manager vs Apple School Manager" |
| *The N-minute check* | Actionable, no commitment | "The 10-minute Mac security check" |
| *What [X] actually costs* | Number in the headline | "What a single lost Mac actually costs you" |

## Decisions made (Aug 2026)

- **Standalones are allowed.** A hook that is useful to three or more verticals, or to none specifically, goes in a new `quick-reads` vertical rather than being force-fitted. Of the first batch of 18, seven carry `quick-reads`.
- **A `ripeda-hook-article` skill now exists** (`skills/ripeda-hook-article/`), with a `validate-hook.py` checker alongside it. It documents seven hook patterns, the structure, the draft-vs-publish modes, and the batch workflow. It inherits voice and banned-phrase rules from `ripeda-insight-article` rather than restating them.
- **Drafts live in `hook-drafts/` at the repo root**, not in `_insights/`. Anything inside the collection generates a page whether or not it is linked. `hook-drafts` needs an `exclude:` line in `_config.yml`.
- **`format: hook`** in `_data/insights.yml` marks the short pieces so the hub can render them distinctly.

## Implementation notes

- Batch-writing is the point. Five or six hooks in a sitting is realistic; five spokes is not.
- Hooks get shared more than spokes, so the default OG card is a bigger loss here. Worth a simple numbered template before the first batch goes live.
- After a quarter live, check Search Console. If a hook outranks its parent spoke, the spoke's title is probably wrong.
