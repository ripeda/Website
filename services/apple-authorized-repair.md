## What an Apple Authorized Service Provider actually is

An Apple Authorized Service Provider (AASP) is a service provider Apple has certified to perform warranty and out-of-warranty repairs using genuine Apple parts, official Apple diagnostic tools, and Apple-defined procedures. Repairs performed at an AASP preserve the device warranty. Repairs performed at unauthorised third-party shops usually do not.

The certification is not casual. AASPs have to meet ongoing Apple operational standards, employ technicians with current Apple credentials (Apple Certified Repair Technician, or ACRT), pass periodic Apple audits, and maintain genuine-parts inventory and equipment. The certification is what separates a fleet repair partner from a generic third-party shop that has bolted a logo onto its window.

## Who AAR is for

RIPEDA's AAR service is available exclusively to managed clients. We do not take walk-in repairs from the public or one-off repair work from organisations we do not have a broader relationship with. The reason is not that we cannot do the work. It is that the value of fleet repair comes from the relationship around it: knowing the device, the user, the configuration, the warranty state, the AppleCare status, the loaner pool the school keeps on a shelf, and the operational context of why this repair matters at this moment.

For a one-off repair, an Apple Store is usually the right answer. For a fleet of Apple devices that needs to keep working, an integrated AAR relationship inside a managed engagement is.

## The repair workflow at fleet scale

The repair workflow at fleet scale looks different from the retail experience. Batched intake. Scheduled pickup or drop-off. Invoices and statements of work for each repair that the client can drop into their own asset records. Apple's own systems track repair history against each device serial number, and any replacement device auto-enrols into Apple Business Manager or Apple School Manager so it is managed from first boot. Direct billing to the organisation's account. A single point of contact at RIPEDA who knows the fleet, not a queue ticket at a mall counter.

For iPads specifically, we treat most repairs as swap-unit replacement rather than component-level repair. The damaged unit comes in, Apple ships a replacement swap unit through our AASP channel, and the swap unit gets configured and returned to the client. This is faster and more reliable than component-level iPad repair, and it avoids the lab investment that component-level work on Apple's current generation of iPads would require. For Macs, repair is done at the component level. Parts are ordered against the specific device for any repair, with lead time depending on what the part is and where it ships from. As an Apple Authorized Service Provider, most of this work stays in-house and never goes to a third-party depot.

## AppleCare for institutional fleets

AppleCare for institutional fleets changes the per-unit repair economics significantly. The math depends on the device class.

Out-of-warranty screen replacement on a current-generation iPad runs into the hundreds of dollars per device. AppleCare service charge on the same repair is much less. For a fleet seeing ten or more screen incidents a year, the AppleCare premium pays for itself with room to spare. Out-of-warranty MacBook repairs are similarly variable; AppleCare service charges are far more predictable.

AppleCare for institutions also covers accidental damage at a reduced service price. The organisation does not negotiate with each staff member or parent over fault. The claim gets filed, the service charge gets paid, the device goes for repair. Battery service is included when capacity drops below the threshold, which matters for three- and four-year-old devices that would otherwise need out-of-warranty battery work.

AppleCare has to be enrolled within the eligibility window after device purchase, which is the most common reason organisations end up paying full out-of-warranty pricing later. The enrollment window is procedural and non-negotiable. We help clients build AppleCare into the standard procurement checklist so the savings get captured automatically.

## Data recovery when the drive fails

Not every repair is a hardware swap. Sometimes the device is intact but the storage in it has failed, and the question is not whether we can repair the Mac but whether we can recover the data on it. For RIPEDA clients, this is where the DriveSavers relationship matters.

RIPEDA is a DriveSavers authorised reseller. When a Mac drive fails, we coordinate the recovery rather than handing the client off cold to a third party. For older Macs with separable storage, the drive can go to the DriveSavers clean-room lab while the Mac stays at RIPEDA for any other repair the device needs. For Apple Silicon Macs with storage integrated into the System on Chip (SoC), the whole unit usually has to go to DriveSavers for the recovery work. DriveSavers operates under Apple's policies for authorised data recovery, so their work does not void manufacturer warranty. RIPEDA arranges the logistics. The recovery itself bills directly from DriveSavers to the client, with a 10% discount applied through RIPEDA's reseller relationship.

## School and business fleet specifics

The repair patterns vary by the kind of fleet the client runs.

School fleets hit damage rates that overwhelm the Apple Store retail model. A typical K-9 school with a one-to-one iPad program sees dozens of cracked screens per year, charging-port damage, and the occasional crushed enclosure. The Apple Store model handles each one as a separate transaction. The AAR model handles them as a fleet, with scheduled twice-weekly pickup, school-owned spare units kept on a shelf for immediate swap, repair logs that feed into the school's asset records, and direct billing to the school account rather than to parents at a retail counter.

Business fleets share the operational pattern but with different damage signatures. Senior staff devices that need to be back in service the same day. Devices in client-facing environments where downtime is highly visible. Compliance-driven environments (legal, accounting, medical) where the loss of a device is a privacy issue in addition to a productivity issue. The AAR workflow for business clients is built around fast turnaround, loaner devices where the contract includes them, direct billing to the organisation, and the kind of relationship-led service that an Apple Store retail counter cannot provide.

For both kinds of fleet, the repair history that accumulates over time becomes its own asset. The monthly repair log shows which devices are being repaired most often, which models are aging fastest, and where the next refresh cycle should be planned. The data feeds directly into lifecycle planning conversations rather than being thrown away after each incident.

## How an engagement works

Apple Authorized Repair operates continuously as part of the managed engagement, not as a discrete project. The intake flow is direct. The client reports a damaged device through our support channel and sends it to RIPEDA via the agreed pickup or drop-off process. We inspect the device, confirm warranty and AppleCare status, and provide a written quote regardless of whether the repair is in-warranty or out-of-warranty. The client either approves the work or declines (most commonly when an out-of-warranty repair is not economical against the age of the device). Whether the repair is completed or declined, the device returns on the next cycle, with the invoice and statement of work for the client's records.

Billing depends on warranty status. In-warranty repairs are covered by AppleCare. Out-of-warranty repairs are billed at a flat per-incident rate, the same flat-rate model an Apple Store uses for the equivalent repair. Data recovery through DriveSavers, when needed, bills directly from DriveSavers to the client at a 10% discount through RIPEDA's reseller relationship.
