A school is a different kind of customer than a business. The budgets cycle annually. The decisions involve a board, a business manager, a headmaster or principal, and often a parent volunteer. The classroom teachers are not IT customers and should not be asked to act like them. And the in-house IT capacity at any given school is almost always small.

RIPEDA's education practice is deliberately scoped. We do not run school help desks or manage individual student and staff devices day-to-day. We do not displace in-house school IT teams. We do not compete with the larger K-12 IT consortiums and Alberta Education service providers who own the bulk of the public-school technology relationship. What we do is provide work that requires specific Apple and network expertise: Apple Authorized Repair on school-owned Macs and iPads, network and infrastructure projects on school campuses, ongoing managed network services for schools whose networks we have built or refreshed, and advisory engagements around Apple School Manager (or Apple Business Manager) and identity for the schools that need it.

The sections below describe the work we do well for schools and how we approach it.

## What we do for schools, and what we do not

The honest version of the scope is short. We repair school-owned Apple devices. We design, install, and manage school network infrastructure end-to-end. We advise on Apple School Manager (or Apple Business Manager) setup and on the identity architecture that sits underneath a school's Mobile Device Management (MDM) platform. We work with school IT leads to plan summer infrastructure projects and capital improvements.

We do not run school help desks, manage individual student or staff devices day-to-day, or take on full IT outsourcing for school clients. That is intentional. The schools we work with have in-house IT teams or contracted IT relationships that already cover the device-level day-to-day. We are the specialist they call in for the Apple-specific work and for the network layer that their generalist setup does not cover.

The result is a clean engagement model. Defined scope. Defined deliverables. No ambiguity about whose desk a given problem belongs on.

## Apple Authorized Repair for school fleets

A school with two hundred iPads and forty Macs has a repair flow problem that consumer Apple retail cannot solve. Devices cannot be dropped off at a mall on a school day. AppleCare claims need to be filed at scale. Batch repair cycles need to be scheduled around teaching calendars rather than around appointment availability.

RIPEDA's Apple Authorized Service Provider status covers the technical side. Warranty repair, AppleCare claims, and out-of-warranty work all happen at our facility with genuine Apple parts. The school side is the logistics: batched pickups, scheduled drop-offs, documented repair logs that fit into the school's existing asset records, and turnaround times that respect a teaching calendar.

AppleCare for schools is its own conversation. Most schools that buy iPads in bulk either purchased AppleCare+ at acquisition or did not, and the difference shows up at the three-year mark when devices start failing in normal wear patterns. We help schools understand what their existing AppleCare coverage actually covers, what the per-incident cost looks like without it, and how to budget for repair as a predictable line item rather than a quarterly surprise.

## Refreshing legacy school infrastructure on a small budget

Most independent schools were not designed around their network. The cabling was installed when the building was last renovated, the wireless was added in pieces as classrooms went one-to-one with iPads, and the switching is whatever the school could afford at the time. By the time a school is ready to refresh, the infrastructure has accumulated a decade of pragmatic decisions that do not add up to a coherent design.

A working school network in 2026 needs business-grade wireless that handles a classroom of forty students on iPads simultaneously, structured cabling that does not require rework every time a classroom is reconfigured, a firewall that supports content filtering and compliance, and a defined refresh cycle so the next ten years do not repeat the last ten. Budgets are real. The work has to be scoped to fit them.

For schools whose networks we design and install, RIPEDA also offers ongoing managed network services. Monitoring, firmware updates, security patches, and configuration changes are handled remotely with a defined service level. The school's in-house IT team retains ownership of device-level support, while RIPEDA takes the network layer off their plate. The split keeps responsibilities clean and keeps the network in the hands of the people who built it.

## Charter, private, and independent schools

Public schools in Alberta sit inside a different IT ecosystem than charter, private, and independent schools. The technology decisions get made by a smaller team with less central infrastructure to lean on, and the Apple footprint is often more significant because the school has more autonomy in platform choice.

## Apple School Manager, Apple Business Manager, and identity advisory

Most schools who run Apple devices have either Apple School Manager (ASM) or Apple Business Manager (ABM) registered, but very few have it set up correctly. ASM is the right tenant for accredited K-12 schools and for higher education institutions that qualify for Apple's education programs. ABM is the equivalent for private K-12 schools that do not meet ASM accreditation, for smaller post-secondary institutions outside the education program, and for the parent corporation of a school that needs a separate business tenant. The two are nearly identical in device management capabilities. ASM adds education-specific features (Apple Classroom, Schoolwork integration, separate student and teacher account types) that ABM does not.

The common gaps are the same in both. Devices purchased before the tenant existed and never retroactively enrolled. Identity that is fragmented across personal Apple IDs and Managed Apple Accounts. An MDM that is configured but not connected through to the institutional account properly.

A short advisory engagement (often a single week of focused work) fixes these gaps. Devices get enrolled retroactively. Managed Apple Accounts replace personal sign-ins. The MDM gets reconnected through the institutional account so future purchases enrol automatically. The school's in-house IT team takes over the day-to-day from there, with the foundation correctly installed.

The identity question is the part most schools underestimate. A school with five hundred students, eighty staff, and a one-to-one iPad program needs three separate identity tiers: student accounts that follow the student through their years at the school, staff accounts that look more like a typical business identity, and shared classroom-device accounts for the cases where a device is used by different students throughout the day. Getting these tiers separated correctly inside ASM (or ABM) and the MDM is the work that makes the rest of the year-round operations sane.

## The school-specific stack

The technology stack inside an Alberta school typically includes a learning management system (Brightspace, PowerSchool, or a smaller specialist platform), Google Workspace for Education or Microsoft 365 for student email and document sharing, an MDM (Jamf School and Mosyle Manager are common in this segment), and the network infrastructure that ties it all together. Apple School Manager (for accredited K-12 and qualifying post-secondary) or Apple Business Manager (for institutions that do not qualify for ASM) sits underneath the MDM as the institutional identity for school-owned devices.

The non-technical context matters too. Privacy obligations under Alberta's Personal Information Protection Act and the Education Act shape what data can live where and how. Parent communication tooling, classroom assessment platforms, and the various subject-specific apps all sit alongside the Apple stack. We work with what is already in place rather than recommending a wholesale replacement that the school cannot operate after we leave.

Each engagement starts with a scoped conversation about what the school needs and whether it fits what we do. Repair engagements run continuously and are billed per device.
