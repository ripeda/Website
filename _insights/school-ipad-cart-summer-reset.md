The carts come back from the last week of classes and get pushed into a storage room. What happens to them between then and the first staff day in September decides how much of your fall is spent on triage.

These are in sequence rather than in order of importance, because each one genuinely depends on the one before it.

## 1. Count every device against the asset list

Open the carts, read serial numbers, and reconcile them against the asset register and the device list in your MDM. July is the month a missing iPad is still findable, because it is in a teacher's desk drawer or a classroom cupboard rather than gone. Anything that does not reconcile now becomes a September write-off, at which point nobody remembers who had it.

## 2. Triage the damage into warranty, repair, and write-off

Sort the damaged units into three piles: covered under AppleCare or warranty, worth paying to repair, and past economic repair. Get that queue moving in early July, not late August, because a screen or battery job competing with every other school's fleet in the last two weeks of summer is the one that arrives in October. An Apple Authorized service provider can confirm coverage and quote the batch before you commit any of the budget.

## 3. Check which models have aged out of iPadOS support

Match every model in the fleet against the current iPadOS release and mark the ones that can no longer take it. Devices stuck on an older major version stop getting security updates and start falling out of compatibility with the classroom apps, and you want that list before enrollment rather than after a teacher reports an app that will not install. It is also the list that justifies next year's purchase request.

## 4. Wipe and re-enrol through Apple School Manager

Erase each device so it comes back through Apple School Manager onto a known configuration instead of another year of accumulated drift. Before you start, confirm the devices are still assigned to the right MDM server in ASM and check the APNs certificate expiry date, because an expired certificate in September stops management traffic across the whole fleet at once. Any device that picked up a personal Apple Account along the way may show Activation Lock, and July is a much better time to sort that out.

## 5. Test the carts, the cables, and the power supplies

Charging carts are consumables that nobody budgets for. Plug every bay, confirm each cable actually charges rather than just seating, and note any cart where the internal power supply or timer board has quietly failed. A cart that charges twenty of thirty iPads overnight presents as a device problem in September and costs a week to diagnose.

## If you only do one thing

Start the repair queue in the first half of July. It is the only item on this list with a lead time you do not control.
