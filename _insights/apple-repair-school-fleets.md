A private K-9 school in Calgary has 240 iPads across the building. By February of any given year, the repair log shows roughly forty cracked screens, eight devices with charging-port damage, and three with bent enclosures from being sat on or otherwise crushed. The Apple Store at the local mall takes most of these in for repair, one at a time, with a parent or staff member dropping them off and picking them up a day or two later. The math works at low volume. At forty devices per term, the math starts costing the school more in labour and downtime than the parts themselves.

This is the operational gap that an Apple Authorized Service Provider (AASP) relationship closes. The school does not need an AASP because the Apple Store cannot do the work. The school needs an AASP because the Apple Store retail model is not structured for fleet volume. AASPs are.

## What an AASP actually is

An AASP is a service provider Apple has certified to perform warranty and out-of-warranty repairs using genuine Apple parts, diagnostic tools, and procedures. Repairs at an AASP preserve the device warranty. Repairs at an unauthorised third-party shop usually do not.

For a school fleet, the meaningful differences from the Apple Store retail experience are:

**Fleet-aware logistics.** AASPs accept batched submissions, often with pickup arrangements, and return repaired devices on a known schedule.

**Loaner and swap inventory.** Some AASPs maintain loaner pools. For iPad fleets specifically, the more reliable pattern is the school keeping four or five spare units on a shelf so a teacher gets a working device in their hands the same hour the original goes for repair.

**Direct billing.** Repair charges go to the school's account rather than to a parent's credit card at a retail counter.

**Volume relationships.** AASPs serving schools usually have a single point of contact for the IT lead or principal, not a queue ticket at a mall counter.

## The AppleCare math for schools

AppleCare for institutional fleets is sold through Apple Authorized Resellers and through Apple's direct education program. It changes the per-unit repair economics in three ways.

**Cracked-screen pricing.** Out-of-warranty screen replacement on a tenth-generation iPad runs roughly four hundred dollars in 2026. AppleCare service charge on the same repair is forty-nine dollars. For a fleet seeing thirty-plus screen incidents a year, the AppleCare premium pays for itself with room to spare.

**Accidental damage coverage.** AppleCare for institutions covers accidental damage at the reduced service price. The school does not negotiate with each parent over fault. The school files a claim, pays the service charge, and moves on.

**Battery service.** Three-year-old iPads in heavy classroom use commonly hit battery degradation. AppleCare includes battery service when capacity drops below the threshold. Outside of AppleCare, battery replacement is an out-of-warranty repair priced like a screen.

AppleCare for schools must be enrolled within sixty days of device purchase, which is the most common reason schools end up paying full out-of-warranty pricing later. The enrollment window is procedural, not negotiable. A school that adds AppleCare into the standard procurement checklist captures the savings automatically.

## What turnaround actually looks like

Three operational details matter more than headline repair pricing. All three depend on the device class.

**Turnaround time.** For iPads, common repairs (screen replacements, port damage, battery service) typically run about five business days from the moment the school approves the repair quote to a repaired device leaving the AASP. iPads that need work beyond a port or screen are usually handled by a swap unit ordered through Apple rather than a component-level repair, which is faster and more reliable than a depot exchange. For Macs, screens and ports run a similar five to seven days, while logic-board and enclosure work runs longer because parts are ordered against the specific device. As an Apple Authorized Service Provider (AASP), most of this work stays in-house and never goes to depot. Schools that approve quotes the day they arrive shave days off every timeline.

**Loaner and swap inventory.** Two complementary pools cover repair gaps. The AASP keeps a small reserve for the cases where on-site swap is not practical. The school keeps its own swap pool, typically four to six spare units for a 240-iPad fleet, sized to cover the gap between when a device breaks and when the next courier run picks it up. A device that breaks on a Friday afternoon does not wait until the Tuesday pickup to be replaced if the school has a spare on the shelf. The math is modest: one to two percent of the fleet on hand is usually enough for routine turnover.

**Shipping and pickup logistics.** A scheduled twice-weekly pickup of damaged devices, with return of the previous batch at the same time, is more efficient than ad-hoc parent runs. The school IT lead or office manager assembles the batch on a known cadence. The AASP plans its repair workflow around the same cadence. Together this means no school staff member is making one-off trips to a service centre for individual devices.

## Where this fits in a school

An AASP relationship is not a full IT contract. It is a focused service line that handles fleet repair and device lifecycle without requiring the school to also outsource its day-to-day technology operations. For charter and private schools without an in-house IT lead, that focus is what makes the relationship useful. The school keeps internal control of the things only the school can decide. The AASP handles the things only an Apple-authorised partner can do.

## When to think about this

The right time to set up an AASP relationship is before the next school year, before the next device refresh, or before the next cracked-screen incident that takes a teacher offline for a week. Most schools that move to AASP partnership do so after a damage event that exposed the limits of the Apple Store retail model.

The relationship pays back the first time a teacher gets a loaner the same day their iPad is dropped off. After that, the case for the partnership writes itself.
