The drive still works. Files open, people find things eventually, and nothing has been lost outright. What has changed is the amount of human effort standing between someone and the file they need.

These five signs tend to arrive in this order, and the earlier ones are cheaper to fix than the later ones.

## 1. The filenames are doing the version control

A file called final, then final2, then FINAL-v3-USE-THIS is not sloppiness, it is a version history that people built by hand because the drive does not keep one. Once the filename is the only record of which file is current, the person who last saved it is the only reliable source of truth. Everyone else is guessing from a string of characters.

## 2. Nobody searches any more, they ask

Watch how someone actually finds a file. If the first move is a message to a colleague rather than a search field, searching has become slower than interrupting somebody. Spotlight does not index most network shares the way it indexes a local disk, so a lookup that takes seconds on a MacBook can take minutes over SMB. The real cost is not the wait, it is that two people are now doing one person's lookup.

## 3. Nobody can say who has access

Ask who can open a given client folder. If the honest answer requires opening the sharing settings folder by folder, the access model is not a model. It is an accumulation of decisions nobody recorded. That becomes a live problem the first time a client's security questionnaire asks for a list of everyone who can reach their material.

## 4. The same asset lives in four places

Somebody needed the logo pack, could not find it in under a minute, and copied it into their own project folder. That is a rational decision for one person and a structural problem when fifteen people make it independently. You end up with four copies that were identical on the day they were created and have since drifted, with no way to tell which one the client approved.

## 5. A new hire needs a person, not a structure

This one is the most expensive, and it is the last to show up. When onboarding a designer means assigning somebody to explain where things live, the structure has stopped carrying the knowledge. The knowledge is in a person's head, and it leaves when they do. A drive a competent new hire cannot navigate on day two has outgrown itself, whatever its capacity says.

## If you only do one thing

Open the drive and find the approved final files from a project that closed six months ago. How long that takes, and how many people you have to ask, is the whole diagnosis.
